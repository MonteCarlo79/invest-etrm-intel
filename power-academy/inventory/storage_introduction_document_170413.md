## Concepts
- **Energy arbitrage** — Shifting electricity from low-price periods to high-price periods to capture wholesale price spreads.
- **System services** — Grid-level services provided by storage covering stabilization (seconds-minutes), capacity (minutes-hours), and supply/demand shift (hours).
- **Power-to-energy ratio** — The ratio of rated power (kW) to energy capacity (kWh) that characterises the discharge duration of a storage technology.
- **Stabilization services** — Short-duration (seconds-minutes) ancillary services that continuously match supply and demand to maintain power quality and frequency.
- **Capacity services** — Medium-duration (minutes-hours) services that provide dispatchable power capacity to the system when needed.
- **Supply/demand shift** — Long-duration (hours) services that move energy from surplus periods to deficit periods to improve the match between variable supply and demand.
- **Pumped Hydro Storage (PHS)** — Mature, geography-constrained bulk storage technology currently the dominant deployed EES capacity worldwide.
- **Compressed Air Energy Storage (CAES)** — Demonstrated large-scale mechanical storage technology that has seen limited efficiency improvement over several decades.
- **Lithium-ion batteries** — High-power-to-energy-ratio electrochemical storage technology currently cost-competitive primarily for short-duration stabilization services.
- **Flow batteries** — Electrochemical storage technology with decoupled power and energy ratings suited to longer-duration load-shifting applications.
- **Levelized Cost of Storage (LCoS)** — A first-order metric expressing the total lifecycle cost of providing a storage service in $/MWh to enable cross-technology comparison.
- **CAPEX thresholds** — Technology-cost benchmarks (in $/kWh installed) below which a storage technology becomes cost-competitive for a given service category.
- **Service stacking (value stacking)** — Delivering multiple system services from a single storage installation to maximise revenue and reduce dependence on any single income stream.
- **Demand response** — Load-side flexibility option that competes with EES in providing certain system services.
- **Interconnectors** — Transmission links between regions that act as competing flexibility resources to storage for cross-border energy balancing.
- **Variable renewable energy (VRE) integration** — The challenge of accommodating non-dispatchable wind and solar output within the power system, driving demand for flexibility services.
- **Behind-the-meter storage** — Customer-sited storage whose value derives from reducing grid-interaction costs such as peak demand charges, explicitly excluded from the main analysis.
- **Round-trip efficiency** — The ratio of energy discharged to energy charged, determining parasitic losses and charging costs over a storage cycle.
- **Module scale** — The physical and power/energy size of a single deployable storage unit, affecting geographic flexibility and grid connection level.
- **Battery pack vs. total installed cost** — The distinction between cell/module costs and full system costs, where battery pack costs represent approximately half of total installed cost.
