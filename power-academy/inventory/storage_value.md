## Concepts
- **Hydro-thermal coordination** — Joint scheduling of hydro and thermal plant to minimise fuel and shortage costs over time.
- **Marginal water value** — Shadow price of stored water reflecting its opportunity cost in offsetting future thermal fuel expenditure.
- **Water value surface** — Multi-dimensional mapping of marginal water value as a function of reservoir storage level and time period.
- **Cournot oligopoly** — Strategic quantity-setting game among generating firms where each firm maximises profit taking rivals' quantities as given.
- **Supply Function Equilibrium (SFE)** — Nash equilibrium in which firms commit to price-quantity supply schedules rather than fixed prices or quantities.
- **Long-term financial contracts** — Forward or option contracts that fix a quantity at a strike price, altering firms' effective marginal revenue and output incentives.
- **Price Distortion Index (PDI)** — Metric quantifying the deviation of equilibrium spot price from the competitive marginal-cost benchmark.
- **Demand Curve for Release (DCR)** — Derived schedule mapping hydro release quantity to a range of marginal water values for use in DDP optimisation.
- **Dual Dynamic Programming (DDP)** — Dual variant of stochastic dynamic programming that iterates on marginal value functions rather than primal state trajectories to solve multi-period reservoir scheduling.
- **Stochastic inflows** — Random hydrological inflows to reservoirs modelled probabilistically to capture uncertainty in future water availability.
- **Competitive fringe** — Price-taking generators whose capacity is netted from market demand before strategic Cournot firms determine equilibrium output.
- **Reaction functions** — Best-response correspondences giving each firm's profit-maximising output as a function of rival firms' outputs.
- **Market Response Curve** — Aggregate equilibrium relationship between total market output and the exogenous marginal cost or water value parameter.
- **Consumer surplus** — Welfare measure capturing the difference between consumers' willingness to pay and the equilibrium spot price paid.
- **Constant elasticity demand curve** — Inverse demand specification in which price elasticity is constant at all output levels, used as an alternative to linear demand.
- **Linear demand curve** — Inverse demand specification with constant slope, yielding linear marginal revenue curves for strategic firms.
- **Storage trajectory** — Time path of reservoir storage levels generated by simulating operating rules derived from the DDP optimisation.
- **Perfect competition benchmark** — Reference case in which all firms price at marginal cost, used to measure efficiency losses under oligopolistic behaviour.
- **Medium-term scheduling** — Weekly-interval, annual-horizon optimisation of generation dispatch and reservoir releases given expected demand and inflows.
- **Market equilibrium** — Simultaneous solution of all firms' best-response conditions that clears the spot market at a single clearing price.
- **Dual Dynamic Programming (DSDP)** — Multi-period stochastic optimisation method for hydro reservoir scheduling that yields water value surfaces and operating guidelines.
- **Water value** — Marginal value of stored water in a reservoir, used as a shadow price to link hydro dispatch decisions across time.
- **Nash equilibrium** — Strategy profile in which no firm can increase its own profit by unilaterally deviating from its equilibrium action.
- **Supply function equilibrium** — Game-theoretic framework in which firms bid entire supply functions rather than scalar quantities or prices.
- **Two-way contract (contract for differences)** — Financial hedge in which a generator and consumer exchange payments based on the difference between the spot price and an agreed strike price on a fixed quantity.
- **Call option (back-up contract)** — One-way contract in which the seller compensates the buyer when the spot price exceeds the strike price on the contracted quantity.
- **Put option** — One-way contract in which the buyer compensates the seller when the spot price falls below the strike price on the contracted quantity.
- **Market response curve (MRC)** — Locus of Cournot Nash equilibrium price-quantity pairs traced out across a range of demand curves for a given period.
- **Reaction function** — Mapping that gives each firm's profit-maximising output as a function of the aggregate output of rival firms.
- **Linear inverse demand curve** — Demand specification expressing market price as an affine function of total generation, used to derive closed-form Cournot equilibria.
- **Price elasticity of demand** — Ratio of the proportional change in quantity demanded to the proportional change in price, used to parameterise demand curves.
- **Stepped marginal cost curve** — Piecewise-constant representation of a firm's marginal production cost as a function of output, derived from unit capacities and heat rates.
- **Admissible equilibrium solution** — A solved Cournot equilibrium point whose implied generation level falls within the generation range consistent with the assumed marginal cost segment.
- **Uniqueness of Cournot equilibrium** — Theoretical result establishing conditions—non-decreasing marginal costs and sufficiently elastic demand—under which exactly one Nash equilibrium exists.
- **Perfect competitor** — Generating firm that bids at true marginal cost and treats market price as exogenous, providing the welfare-benchmark dispatch.
- **Nett spot generation** — A firm's total generation minus its contracted quantity, determining whether it is a net buyer or seller in the spot market.
- **Dispatch market** — Half-hourly clearing mechanism in which generators submit stepped offer curves and purchasers submit bid curves to set dispatch quantities and prices.
- **Market share relationship** — Formula expressing each firm's equilibrium generation as an equal share adjusted for the deviation of its marginal cost from the industry average.
- **Single aggregate reservoir model** — Simplification that represents all hydro storage as one reservoir, ignoring spatial, transmission and head-effect complexities.
- **Reserve capacity market** — Ancillary-services market requiring plant capable of responding within specified times to cover unexpected generation failures.
- **Cournot oligopoly equilibrium** — A non-cooperative quantity-setting game solution where each firm maximises profit given rivals' outputs, used to determine spot-market generation and price.
- **Two-way (forward) contracts** — Financial instruments fixing a price for a quantity of electricity that reduce a firm's incentive to raise the spot price above its contract strike price.
- **Call (one-way / back-up) contracts** — Options transferring contract obligations from one firm to another when the spot price exceeds the strike price, affecting the effective contracting level of each firm.
- **Perfectly competitive (PC) benchmark** — The reference dispatch in which every station generates whenever its marginal cost is below the spot price, yielding the minimum-cost merit-order solution.
- **Merit order and plant portfolio effects** — The observation that a firm owning low-marginal-cost, large-capacity plant has greater ability to raise the spot price than a firm owning high-merit-order plant.
- **Demand elasticity sensitivity** — The dependence of market-power distortion on the slope of the demand curve, with less elastic (steeper) demand producing greater price mark-ups.
- **Demand Curve for Storage (DCS)** — A schedule mapping marginal water value to optimal end-of-period reservoir storage, derived recursively by aggregating the DCR with the future DCS and inflow distributions.
- **Stochastic inflow treatment** — Incorporation of uncertain future inflows as a discrete probability distribution, producing an expected DCS via probability-weighted averaging of shifted value curves.
- **Storage bounds and spill / shortage costs** — Physical upper and lower limits on reservoir storage that truncate the DCS at zero (spill) or a fixed shortage penalty (minimum storage), bounding marginal water values.
- **Market breakup / asset allocation** — The assignment of generation stations among competing firms, which determines each firm's portfolio, market share, and consequent ability to exercise market power.
- **Allocative vs productive efficiency trade-off** — The distinction between minimising total generation cost (productive efficiency) and maximising consumer surplus (allocative efficiency), which back-up contracts can affect in opposing directions.
- **Monopolist vs price-taker mixed market** — A market structure where one firm acts as a Cournot monopolist and the other dispatches competitively, producing non-monotone MRCs and soft price caps at each competitive firm's marginal cost step.
- **Load Duration Curve (LDC)** — A representation of electricity demand ranked by level over a period, used to weight generation decisions and translate single-period results to a longer planning horizon.
- **Water Value Surface (WVS)** — A two-dimensional surface mapping the marginal value of stored water as a function of storage level and time period.
- **Marginal Water Value (MWV)** — The shadow price of water in storage, used as the effective fuel cost of hydro generation in dispatch decisions.
- **Cournot duopoly with competitive fringe** — A single-period market model where two strategic firms choose quantities simultaneously alongside a price-taking fringe.
- **Forward/financial contracts and back-up contracts** — Pre-committed energy obligations that alter firm incentives, reducing price distortion as contract coverage increases toward 100%.
- **Stochastic inflow uncertainty** — Random variation in reservoir inflows handled by probability-weighted averaging of shifted DCSs within the DDP framework.
- **Merit order and hydro scheduling** — The ranking of generation units by marginal cost, with MWV determining where hydro sits relative to thermal plant in dispatch.
- **Reservoir storage bounds and spill risk** — Physical limits on reservoir capacity that constrain optimal release policy and create spill risk at low contract levels.
- **Demand elasticity** — The price-responsiveness of electricity demand, which governs the magnitude of price distortion in the Cournot market model.
- **Consumer and producer surplus** — Welfare measures used to evaluate outcomes across different contracting regimes and market structures.
- **Load duration curve (LDC) sub-period modelling** — Representation of intra-period demand variation via sub-periods whose DCRs are combined as a convex-weighted average.
- **Monotonicity of DCR and DCS** — Theoretical property guaranteeing that both the demand curve for release and the demand curve for storage are non-increasing, ensuring DDP validity.
- **Forward contracts** — Financial instruments committing generators to supply electricity at pre-agreed prices, including one-way and two-way variants as well as put and call options.
- **Hydro reservoir scheduling** — Optimal inter-temporal allocation of water releases from reservoirs to generate electricity subject to inflow uncertainty.
- **Stochastic dynamic programming** — A recursive optimisation framework for sequential decision-making under uncertainty, applied here to reservoir release scheduling.
- **Oligopoly pricing** — Strategic price or quantity setting by a small number of electricity generating firms, encompassing Cournot and Bertrand solution concepts.
- **Cournot outcome** — A Nash equilibrium in quantities resulting from capacity precommitment followed by price competition, as in the Kreps-Scheinkman framework.
- **Back-up capacity** — Thermal generation capacity available as a competitive fringe or fallback supply that constrains the market power of hydro-thermal incumbents.
- **Energy spot price** — The short-run market-clearing price for electricity in each period, determined by aggregate supply and demand.
- **Profit distribution** — The mean and variance of firm-level revenues net of costs across Monte Carlo or stochastic simulation draws.
- **Storage level** — The volume of water held in a reservoir at each time step, linking current decisions to future generation capacity.
- **Correlated inflows** — Statistical dependence across time or across reservoirs in the random water inflow process, affecting multi-period scheduling.
- **Deregulated electricity market** — A restructured power sector in which generation is exposed to competitive market forces rather than regulated monopoly dispatch.
- **Quantity precommitment** — A first-stage capacity or output commitment that shapes subsequent price competition and can yield Cournot-equivalent outcomes.
- **Biases in stochastic reservoir models** — Systematic errors arising from discretisation or approximation in stochastic DP models for hydro scheduling.
- **Total contract quantity** — The aggregate volume of forward contracts written by generators, used as the key independent variable in the simulation sensitivity analysis.
- **contract quantity** — The total volume of forward or bilateral contracts written, used as a key parameter varying across simulation scenarios.
- **mean-variance analysis of market outcomes** — Statistical characterisation of equilibrium variables by their means and standard deviations across stochastic realisations.
- **multi-period market equilibrium** — An equilibrium concept spanning several time periods in which storage decisions link current and future market outcomes.
