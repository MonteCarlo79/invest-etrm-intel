## Concepts
- **Electricity storage technology taxonomy** — Classification of storage technologies into five classes: mechanical, chemical, thermal, molecular, and electrical, with sub-categories for each.
- **System services framework** — Categorisation of storage applications into stability, capacity, and energy services, differentiated by discharge time and required power rating.
- **Power-to-energy ratio** — The inherent ratio of rated power to stored energy capacity that characterises each storage technology and determines its natural service match.
- **Use-case stacking** — The practice of dispatching a single storage installation to provide multiple non-coincident services to capture incremental revenue streams.
- **Behind-the-meter storage** — Storage sited on the customer side of the utility meter to reduce demand charges, integrate distributed PV, or improve power quality.
- **Energy arbitrage** — The strategy of charging storage during low-price periods and discharging during high-price periods to capture wholesale price spreads.
- **Ancillary services** — Grid-stability products such as frequency regulation procured by system operators and valued on a per-kW basis.
- **Levelised cost of storage (LCoS)** — A cost metric analogous to LCOE that captures total lifecycle costs per unit of energy delivered by a storage asset, with strong dependence on use case and duration.
- **Storage CAPEX components** — Breakdown of installed battery system cost into battery pack, inverter, balance of plant, EPC, and energy management system elements.
- **Technology learning curves** — Empirical cost-reduction trajectories for storage technologies as cumulative deployed capacity increases, analogous to solar PV experience curves.
- **Flexibility supply curve** — A conceptual merit-order ranking of grid flexibility resources by cost and volume, showing how storage competes with demand response, interconnectors, and thermal generation.
- **Microgrid integration** — The combined operation of distributed PV, battery storage, and dispatchable generation in an islanded or semi-islanded configuration to provide reliable local power.
- **Demand response** — Automated or manual curtailment or shifting of electrical loads to provide grid flexibility, competing directly with stationary storage on cost.
- **Market structure and storage economics** — The influence of wholesale market design, price volatility, ancillary service rates, and regulatory environment on the financial viability of storage projects.
- **NPV-based storage project economics** — Project valuation methodology using discounted cash flows driven by revenue from energy shifting and ancillary services net of capital and operating costs.
- **Li-ion chemistry variants** — Sub-types of lithium-ion batteries differentiated by active cathode material (LFP, NCM, NCA, LCO, LTO, LMO) with distinct energy density and cycle-life trade-offs.
- **Pumped hydro storage (PHS)** — Mature large-scale mechanical storage technology using elevation difference to store and release energy, serving as the baseline long-duration storage benchmark.
- **Compressed air energy storage (CAES)** — Bulk mechanical storage technology compressing air into underground caverns, available in conventional, adiabatic, and isothermal variants.
- **Flow batteries** — Electrochemical storage systems where energy is stored in liquid electrolytes circulated through a cell stack, suited to longer-duration applications.
- **Variable renewable energy (vRES) integration** — Use of storage and other flexibility resources to absorb output variability and forecast error from wind and solar generation.
