## Concepts
- **Wind speed time series** — Hub-height (115 m) wind speed observations recorded at hourly intervals for each simulated period.
- **Wind direction time series** — Hub-height (115 m) wind direction in degrees recorded alongside speed for each hourly interval.
- **Wind turbine power output (kW)** — Instantaneous active power produced by a single wind turbine generator derived from wind speed via a power curve.
- **Wind turbine energy output (kWh)** — Hourly energy generation equal to the average power over each one-hour period, numerically identical to kW for one-hour intervals.
- **Power curve** — The nonlinear relationship mapping hub-height wind speed to turbine electrical output, including cut-in, rated, and cut-out thresholds.
- **Cut-in and cut-out wind speed** — Lower and upper wind speed bounds outside which the turbine produces zero output, observable in the data as zero kW entries.
- **Rated power** — The maximum nameplate capacity of the turbine (~3 MW), reached and held constant above a certain wind speed.
- **Long-run wind resource simulation** — Multi-year (20-year) synthetic or reanalysis-derived hourly wind series used to characterise the statistical distribution of wind generation.
- **Capacity factor** — The ratio of actual energy produced to maximum possible energy over a period, derivable by aggregating the hourly output series.
- **Wind variability and intermittency** — The short-term and seasonal fluctuations in wind speed that cause large swings in turbine output within and across days.
- **Wind direction** — Meteorological bearing in degrees indicating the compass direction from which wind is blowing at each observation interval.
- **Wind speed** — Measured wind velocity in metres per second recorded at hourly sub-intervals for a wind generation site.
- **Wind power output** — Electrical power generated by a wind turbine or farm in kilowatts derived from observed wind speed at each time step.
- **Turbine capacity cap** — Maximum rated power output of the wind installation above which generated power is curtailed or saturated regardless of wind speed.
- **Hourly time series resolution** — Temporal granularity of observations recorded at 30-minute or 1-hour intervals over a multi-month period.
- **Wind power curve** — Nonlinear functional relationship mapping wind speed to power output, exhibiting a cubic region and a rated-power plateau.
- **Capacity constraint / rated power clipping** — The phenomenon whereby power output is capped at the turbine nameplate rating when wind speed exceeds the rated threshold.
- **Half-hourly settlement interval** — The 30-minute metering and trading period implicit in the data timestamps, relevant to GB-style electricity market settlement.
- **Intermittency and variability** — The irregular, weather-driven fluctuation of wind generation output that complicates scheduling and forecasting.
- **Wind turbine power output** — Conversion of measured wind speed into electrical power generation at hourly or sub-hourly resolution.
- **Cut-in and rated speed thresholds** — Wind speed boundaries that determine zero-output, partial-output, and full-rated-output operating regimes for a turbine.
- **Imbalance cost** — Financial penalty or settlement charge arising from deviations between forecast and actual wind generation within a balancing regime.
- **Generation forecast** — Predicted wind power output at turbine or portfolio level used as the basis for energy scheduling.
- **Forecast error** — Difference between forecast and actual generation, driving imbalance exposure for wind assets.
- **Imbalance settlement** — Market mechanism by which deviations from contracted positions are priced and charged to generators.
- **Onshore vs offshore wind economics** — Comparative cost and revenue structure distinguishing land-based from marine wind installations.
- **Portfolio aggregation of wind assets** — Combining individual turbine or farm outputs and forecasts to assess net imbalance at a portfolio level.
- **Sub-hourly generation data** — Wind production records at 15-minute or 30-minute granularity used for high-resolution settlement analysis.
- **Wind farm economics** — Revenue, cost, and profitability assessment for a wind generation project including imbalance charges.
