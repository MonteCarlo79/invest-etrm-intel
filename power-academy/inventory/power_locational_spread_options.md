## Concepts
- **Locational spread option** — An option whose payoff depends on the price difference between power at two geographic locations.
- **Forward model simulation** — Monte Carlo simulation of forward power price curves across multiple maturities for multiple commodities.
- **Spot model simulation** — Simulation of hourly and daily spot power prices derived from forward curves, covering peak, off-peak, and base load segments.
- **Rolling products** — Standardised forward contracts reconstructed from simulated prices to have a fixed time-to-maturity at each simulation date.
- **Single volatility model** — Baseline spot price model assuming constant return volatility, used as a benchmark for likelihood-ratio model selection.
- **GARCH(1,1) model** — Time-varying volatility model for spot power returns where conditional variance follows an autoregressive moving-average process.
- **Jump diffusion model** — Spot price model augmenting continuous diffusion with a Poisson jump process to capture sudden price spikes.
- **Two-regime model with Normal spikes** — Regime-switching spot price model with two states where the spike regime has Normally distributed innovations.
- **Three-regime model with Normal spikes** — Extension of the two-regime model adding a third regime to better capture extreme spike behaviour.
- **Two-regime model with Poisson spikes** — Regime-switching spot price model where spike arrivals within the spike regime are governed by a Poisson process.
- **Likelihood ratio test** — Statistical test comparing nested spot price models to select the best-fitting specification at a given confidence level.
- **Cointegration parameters** — Parameters capturing the long-run equilibrium relationship between UK and French power spot prices.
- **Weekday seasonality weights** — Multiplicative factors adjusting simulated spot prices to reflect systematic intra-week demand patterns.
- **Spread analysis** — Post-simulation computation of statistical properties of price differences between the two power markets.
- **FX rate simulation** — Simulation of EUR/GBP exchange rates to convert French power prices into GBP-denominated equivalents.
- **Realized volatility** — Historical volatility computed from observed price returns and used to calibrate forward model parameters.
- **Simulated implied volatility and correlation** — Volatilities and correlations of rolling forward products derived from Monte Carlo paths for use in option pricing.
- **Locational spread** — The price differential between power at two geographic locations, here UK and French power markets.
- **Spot price regime-switching model** — A stochastic model that allows spot power prices to switch between distinct regimes capturing normal and spike behaviour.
- **GARCH(1,1) volatility model** — A time-varying volatility specification applied to power spot price returns to capture volatility clustering.
- **Cross-market price correlation** — The linear correlation between UK and French spot power price innovations used to model co-movement in the spread.
- **Forward curve simulation** — Monte Carlo generation of forward price paths for base, peak, and off-peak power contracts across future delivery months and years.
- **Rolling forward outputs** — Simulated forward price arrays organised by contract tenor (monthly, yearly) and updated on a rolling basis.
- **Spot-to-forward spread** — The simulated differential between spot and forward power prices used to value spread options between locations.
- **FX rate simulation (EUR/GBP)** — Stochastic simulation of the EUR/GBP exchange rate to convert UK GBP-denominated power prices into EUR for cross-market spread valuation.
- **Peak and off-peak price segmentation** — Separation of power prices into peak-hour and off-peak-hour products to reflect intraday demand and price structure.
- **Hourly spot power simulation** — Generation of simulated hourly spot prices over multi-year horizons for use in plant dispatch and spread option valuation.
- **Carbon (EUA) price input** — EU Emission Allowance futures prices used as an input commodity affecting power generation costs in the simulation model.
- **Coal (API2 ARA) price input** — API2 coal futures prices used as a fuel cost input within the power spot price simulation framework.
- **NBP gas price input** — UK National Balancing Point natural gas futures prices used as a fuel cost driver in the power simulation model.
- **Nodal Price Spread** — The difference in electricity prices between two nodes or hubs arising from transmission constraints and congestion.
- **Congestion Rent** — Revenue generated by the difference in locational marginal prices across a constrained transmission path.
- **Locational Marginal Price (LMP)** — The marginal cost of serving an additional unit of load at a specific network node, incorporating energy, congestion, and loss components.
- **Spread Option Pricing** — Valuation of options written on the difference between two correlated underlying asset prices.
- **Correlation Structure of Power Prices** — The joint statistical dependence between prices at different locations, critical for spread option valuation.
- **Monte Carlo Simulation for Spread Options** — Numerical pricing of spread options via simulation of correlated price paths across multiple nodes.
- **Mean Reversion in Power Prices** — The tendency of electricity spot prices to revert to a long-run equilibrium level, affecting spread dynamics.
- **Price Spike Modeling** — Capturing the extreme upward price excursions observed in electricity markets within a stochastic model.
- **Margrabe Formula** — Closed-form approximation for pricing exchange options (spread options with zero strike) under log-normal assumptions.
- **Kirk's Approximation** — An analytic approximation extending the Margrabe formula to spread options with a non-zero strike.
- **Transmission Congestion Contract (TCC) / Financial Transmission Right (FTR)** — Financial instruments whose payoff mirrors the congestion component of the locational spread, used for hedging transmission risk.
- **Basis Risk** — The residual risk arising from imperfect correlation between prices at two locations when using one location's instrument to hedge another.
- **Stochastic Volatility for Power Spreads** — Models that allow the volatility of the spread process to evolve randomly over time.
- **Regime-Switching Models** — Multi-state models where price dynamics shift between distinct regimes such as normal and spike states.
- **Interconnector** — A transmission link connecting two electricity markets across which power and price differentials can be arbitraged.
- **Spread option valuation** — Pricing of options written on the difference between two correlated underlying price series.
- **Correlation between nodal prices** — The statistical dependence between electricity prices at different locations, which drives spread option value.
- **Price curve alignment** — Matching and synchronising price time series from different markets or settlement periods for joint analysis.
- **Implied volatility surface** — The set of market-implied volatilities across strikes and tenors used to calibrate spread option models.
- **Two-asset log-normal model** — A joint model assuming each location price follows geometric Brownian motion, used as a baseline for spread pricing.
