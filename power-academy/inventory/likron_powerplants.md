## Concepts
- **Clean spread** — The margin derived by subtracting fuel and emission rights costs from the electricity price, used as the core hedging underlying for thermal plant profitability.
- **Static hedge** — A fixed futures position that does not adjust to changing spread levels or plant operation schedules over time.
- **Dynamic hedging** — Continuous adjustment of futures positions in response to spread movements to maximise expected profit within a prescribed risk budget.
- **Hourly resolved operation schedule** — A time-granular plan of plant dispatch identifying which individual hours are profitable given current spread levels and technical constraints.
- **Hedge size** — The quantity of futures contracts held, calibrated to match the expected profitable operating hours of the plant.
- **Risk budget** — A prescribed limit on financial risk exposure within which hedge adjustments must remain.
- **Bid-offer spread** — The transaction cost arising from low liquidity in energy markets that increases the cost of rebalancing futures positions.
- **Calendar base/peak futures** — Standardised futures contracts covering base-load or peak-load delivery over a calendar period used as hedging instruments for generation capacity.
- **Plant technical flexibility** — The operational constraints of a power plant that bound the feasible set of hourly dispatch decisions during schedule optimisation.
- **Back testing** — Validation of hedging strategies against both historical market data and simulated price scenarios to assess performance and robustness.
