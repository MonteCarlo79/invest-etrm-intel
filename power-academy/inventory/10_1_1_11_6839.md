## Concepts
- **Swing option** — An electricity OTC derivative allowing purchased power and energy to vary between contractually specified lower and upper bounds over a delivery period.
- **One-Swing option** — A swing option variant granting a single consumption swing right from the lower to the upper power boundary within the delivery period.
- **Full-Swing option** — A swing option variant where the number of swing rights equals the number of discrete delivery dates in the delivery period.
- **Load pattern** — A stochastic process describing the time-varying power consumption or production quantity subject to energy and power feasibility constraints.
- **Power and energy constraints** — Deterministic bounds on instantaneous power usage and cumulative energy purchased that define the feasible set of load patterns for a swing option.
- **Electricity forward curve** — The time-indexed term structure of forward prices on electricity spot price, used as the primary pricing input and hedging instrument.
- **Swing option decomposition** — Representation of the swing option payoff as a portfolio of a mandatory (forward-hedged) baseline, a supplementary forward component, and an optional call-option component.
- **Forward martingale measure** — The equivalent martingale measure under which the discounted forward price is a martingale, used to price each delivery-date component of the swing option.
- **Call option martingale measure** — The equivalent martingale measure associated with a European call option numeraire, enabling decomposition of optional purchase probabilities into call option values.
- **Optimal load pattern (stopping time problem)** — The constrained stochastic control problem solved by the option holder to maximise swing option value by choosing when to consume at the upper power boundary.
- **Markov load pattern approximation** — A tractable subclass of load patterns in which optimal purchase decisions depend only on current market prices, yielding a lower boundary for the swing option price.
- **Swing option lower boundary** — A closed-form computable lower bound on the true swing option price derived by restricting the optimal load pattern to Markov strategies expressed as indicator functions.
- **Linear optimisation for hedging sets** — A linear programme that determines the time sets over which forwards versus call options are used to replicate the optimal Markov load pattern.
- **Bermudan option characteristics** — The multi-exercise, path-dependent nature of swing options that makes full analytical pricing intractable and motivates the lower-boundary approximation.
- **Black-76 model** — The commodity option pricing formula applied to electricity forward prices to compute European call option values from forward price and volatility data.
- **Complete and arbitrage-free electricity derivative market** — The market assumption that electricity forwards and options span all risk sources and that the law of one price holds, guaranteeing a unique pricing measure.
- **Power plant production optimisation** — Application of the swing option framework to schedule generation output subject to capacity and fuel constraints, treated as holding a swing option with strike equal to production cost.
- **Electricity forward curve volatility** — The term structure of volatilities of forward prices, which governs call option values and therefore the portion of the swing option price attributable to optionality.
- **Wealth equation and replication strategy** — A dynamic self-financing portfolio condition linking the swing option seller's hedging gains and losses to the traded forwards and call options.

## Methods
- No-arbitrage pricing via equivalent martingale measure
- Change of numeraire (forward and call option measures)
- Stochastic control / constrained optimisation over load patterns
- Linear programming for optimal hedging set selection
- Black-76 European call option pricing
- Indicator-function (Markov) approximation for lower boundary
- Semimartingale and Hilbert-space (L2) framework
- Nord Pool forward and volatility curve calibration
- Numerical examples with tabular and graphical sensitivity analysis
